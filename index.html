Rostom Mbarek

Astrophysicist

I'm a theoretical astrophysicist studying the high-energy plasma physics of black holes and their surroundings. I work at the intersection of kinetic plasma physics, particle acceleration and transport, high-energy radiation, and multimessenger astrophysics, connecting microscopic plasma processes to the global accretion flows, jets, and winds through which black holes grow and shape their host galaxies.

Much of my current work centers on the coronae, relativistic jets, and magnetized disks of active galactic nuclei (AGN). These are the regions where the extreme electrodynamics near a supermassive black hole is channeled into radiation, outflows, and energetic particles. High-energy observables, whether photons, cosmic rays, or neutrinos, are a powerful window into black-hole accretion, jet formation, inner-disk structure, and wind launching. To study these phenomena I combine analytic theory with first-principles kinetic (particle-in-cell) and magnetohydrodynamic simulations, and I increasingly apply the same tools to neutron-star mergers and other multimessenger transients.

I received my PhD from the University of Chicago, advised by Damiano Caprioli and Scott Wakely, and was then a Neil Gehrels Prize Fellow at the Joint Space Science Institute (University of Maryland & NASA Goddard). I am currently a Lyman Spitzer Jr. Fellow at Princeton University.

Research

Black-Hole Coronae: Formation, Composition, and Radiation

Coronae are the main X-ray emitters in AGN. They are compact, highly magnetized plasma near the event horizon, where hot electrons inverse-Compton scatter disk photons into X-rays and accelerated ions can produce high-energy neutrinos. Their geometry, particle content, and heating are usually prescribed. My goal is to predict them from the magnetic structure and plasma physics of the inner flow. My kinetic study of NGC 1068 connected coronal proton acceleration to its X-ray and neutrino emission (Mbarek et al. 2024), and a recent model proposes that magnetic flux carried inward by circumnuclear winds accumulates into the compact region that emerges as the X-ray corona (Mbarek 2026a).

Relativistic Jets: Acceleration, Emission, and Structure

Relativistic jets carry energy from the central engine out to galactic scales and are leading candidate sources of ultra-high-energy cosmic rays and neutrinos. I build frameworks to follow particle injection, acceleration, and escape in three-dimensional MHD jet simulations and connect the resulting particle populations to jet emission and multimessenger signals. A current focus is the changing-look AGN 1ES 1927+654, whose newly emerging jet (Meyer et al. 2025; Laha et al. 2025) offers a rare chance to study a corona–jet transition.

High-Energy Neutrinos

Neutrinos are weakly interacting messengers, produced in proton–proton and proton–photon interactions, that let us probe regions photons cannot escape. I predict neutrino emission from both AGN coronae and jets: from the corona of NGC 1068 (Mbarek et al. 2024) and from accelerated ions in AGN jets (Mbarek et al. 2023), and I connect kinetically-informed coronal acceleration to the diffuse extragalactic neutrino background from supermassive black holes (Mbarek 2026b ). Neutron-Star Mergers and Multimessenger Transients

The plasma physics of accretion flows and jets carries over to other strongly magnetized, radiating systems. In the magnetic towers of neutron-star merger remnants, I showed how accelerated protons initiate hadronic cascades that load the outflow with electron–positron pairs, redistribute dissipated magnetic energy, and emit neutrinos (Mbarek, Wu & Most 2026). This reshapes the plasma that later powers the highest-energy emission of short gamma-ray bursts. As genuine multimessenger transients, these systems tie plasma microphysics to the gravitational-wave and time-domain era.

Particle Acceleration & Transport

Relativistic Asymmetric Reconnection

Magnetic reconnection — the breaking and reconnecting of oppositely directed field lines — need not occur between two identical plasmas, as has usually been assumed in the relativistic regime. We established scaling relations for relativistic asymmetric reconnection and tested them against PIC simulations (Mbarek et al. 2022). The image is a zoomed-in 3D PIC simulation of an asymmetric reconnecting layer (credit: Sophia Woznichak). Asymmetric reconnection is prominent in the heliosphere and should be studied in the relativistic regime.

Stochastic Acceleration in Magnetized Turbulence

In magnetically dominated turbulence, I derived and tested a scaling law linking macroscopic plasma properties to the nonthermal particle spectra produced by stochastic acceleration, including the effect of particle escape (Mbarek et al. 2026). This connects the conditions resolved by global simulations to the particle spectra they cannot resolve.

One-Shot Acceleration

I developed a framework, agnostic to the acceleration mechanism, that integrates particle trajectories through relativistic 3D magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations. It showed that ions can reach ultra-high energies via espresso, tapping the motional electric field of the ultrarelativistic jet spine for a boost of order the square of the flow Lorentz factor (Mbarek & Caprioli 2019, ApJ 886, 8).

Stochastic and Shear Reacceleration in Jets

Unresolved small-scale scattering can unlock stochastic reacceleration in relativistic jets (Mbarek & Caprioli 2021 ), and shear acceleration in the Centaurus A jet can imprint itself on the ultra-high-energy neutrino spectrum (Mbarek et al. 2025). These calculations build on a bottom-up framework linking espresso-reaccelerated ions in AGN jets to their ultra-high-energy cosmic-ray and neutrino output (Mbarek et al. 2023).
